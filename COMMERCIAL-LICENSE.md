| | |

| **[AGPL-3.0](LICENSE)** | Free. For open-source work, internal use, research, evaluation — anything where you are willing to publish your own source under the same terms if you distribute or host a work built on this one. |

Nothing here restricts *using* the software. Both licences let you run it, on
as many machines as you like, for any purpose including work you are paid for.
What the AGPL asks for is source, and only when you pass something on.

## When the AGPL is enough

Most people never need a commercial licence. The free licence covers you if:

- you use it to do your own GIS work, paid or unpaid, and keep the results to
  yourself or your client;
- you use it inside a company, on internal projects, without distributing it
  outside;
- you modify it and publish your modifications under the AGPL;
- you build something on it that is itself released under the AGPL.

Consulting work is included. Delivering maps, analysis, or data you produced
with this tool is not distributing the tool.

## When you need a commercial licence

The AGPL's requirement bites when you **convey** the software or make it
available **over a network**. You need a commercial licence to:

- ship it, or anything derived from it, inside a product you do not publish the
  source of;
- host it as part of a service other people use, without publishing the source
  of that service;
- resell it, bundle it, or distribute it under your own name;
- remove or replace this licence for your customers.

If you are unsure which side of the line you are on, ask. A short description
of what you are building usually settles it in one reply.

## Getting one

Open an issue titled **"Commercial licence enquiry"** at
<https://github.com/Knight60/ArcGIS-Pro-MCP/issues>, or contact
[@Knight60](https://github.com/Knight60).

Include roughly:

- what you are building and how this fits into it;
- whether it is distributed to customers, hosted as a service, or both;
- the size of the organisation.

Terms are set case by case. Small teams and single products are treated very
differently from redistribution at scale, and there is no interest in making
this expensive for people doing honest work.

## Contributions

Contributions are accepted under the AGPL-3.0, and by contributing you grant
the copyright holder permission to include your contribution in commercially
licensed copies as well. Without that, dual licensing cannot work — the
copyright holder has to be able to license the whole of it.

If you would rather not grant that, say so in the pull request. Your
contribution can still be discussed, and in most cases the same result can be
reached another way.

## Versions before this change

Releases up to and including **v1.0** were published under the MIT licence.
That grant cannot be withdrawn: anyone who obtained those versions keeps MIT
rights to them, permanently, including the right to use them commercially.

The dual AGPL/commercial licence applies from **v1.1** onward.
